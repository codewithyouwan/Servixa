## Hierarchy & conversion notes

- Hero is deliberately short (320px vs. 600px+ elsewhere) — a pricing page's job is to answer a specific question fast, not build atmosphere; over-designing it delays the answer the visitor came for.
- The homeowner pricing card stands alone, visually separated from the contractor tiers, rather than being one more column in the same table — conflating "free for one audience, tiered for the other" into a single grid would force the $0 homeowner plan to visually compete with contractor tiers it has nothing to do with.
- The middle contractor tier ("Pro") carries the heavy-border treatment as the recommended plan — standard SaaS pricing-page convention (used by Vercel, Notion) to reduce decision paralysis by visually suggesting a default choice.
- The MVP note about premium features being free during early access is placed directly in the pricing block, not hidden in fine print — per the earlier design docs, overstating a mature pricing ladder that doesn't exist yet is a credibility risk once a contractor signs up and finds it different.
